All your money, in one place, adding up.

Two banks, a credit card, PayPal or Satispay, a broker, a crypto exchange, the cash in your pocket. Each app shows you its own slice; Bussola adds them all up: where the money goes, and what it is worth altogether. It runs on a server of your own, and no bank password ever leaves your hands.

Try the demo Install it

The demo runs in your browser on invented data. Nothing you type leaves the tab, and a reload starts it over. Bussola itself is free and open source, MIT licence.

Why Bussola

  1. Every account, one total

    Banks, cards, payment apps, brokers, exchanges and cash are all just accounts. Open new ones and close old ones as often as you like: archive what you no longer use and its history stays.

  2. Spending and net worth together

    Expenses and income month by month, and next to them everything you own: ETFs, stocks, bonds, crypto, deposits, across four pillars, with a snapshot every month.

  3. No aggregator, no bank passwords

    Your phone forwards each bank notification; a language model of your choice turns it into a draft, and you accept it in one tap. Or import any bank's CSV.

  4. Yours, shared on your terms

    Self-hosted and multi-user, multi-wallets: share a wallet with read or read-write permissions. PostgreSQL row-level security keeps wallets apart. No telemetry.

Day to day

Accounts, categories and transactions: what you spend, what you earn and what you move between accounts. The keypad does sums, so 12.40+7.90 is one entry and not a trip to the calculator, and the whole form runs from the keyboard. Search, filter, and page back through years of history.

What you own

ETFs, ETCs, stocks, bonds, funds, crypto and deposit accounts, split across four pillars: cash, emergency fund, planned expenses and long term, with each holding's return and its diversification by type, issuer, country and currency. A snapshot at the start of every month keeps the history. ETF, stock and crypto prices can update by themselves through an optional service that runs next to it.

On a phone, the same app

Install it on the home screen like any app, and get a notification when a bank movement is waiting for you.

Everything it does

Bank notifications become drafts
An Android automation app or an iPhone Shortcut sends each bank notification to Bussola's inbox. A language model of your choice, on your own network or hosted, drops offers and codes and fills in the amount, day, account and category. Accept it in one tap, or review it first.
Notifications on your phone
Install Bussola as an app and it tells you when a draft is waiting: Web Push, from Bussola itself, with no third-party app.
Multi-user, multi-wallets
Everyone has their own account and as many wallets as they need. Share one with read-write permissions with your partner, or read permissions with your accountant. PostgreSQL row-level security, not application code, decides who sees what.
Any bank's CSV
A wizard reads any bank's export, guesses separator, dates and decimals, and shows a preview with likely duplicates before anything is saved.
Your data comes and goes
Export a whole wallet as one archive and import it into another wallet or another installation, balances included.
Automatic prices, if you want them
An optional container fetches ETF, ETC, stock and crypto prices from official APIs, in your wallet's currency. Without it, you type values in.
Bank mode
Press H and every amount on screen blurs, for when someone is looking over your shoulder.
Keyboard first
Shortcuts for nearly everything; press ? to see them. A dark theme, and a layout that works on a phone.
Passkeys
Sign in with a fingerprint or your face. The password stays as a fallback.
Your language, your currency
English, Italian, French, German, Spanish and Chinese, each person their own, and a currency for each wallet.
Small and self-contained
Three Docker containers, one port, images for amd64 and arm64: a Raspberry Pi or a NAS is enough.
Nothing phones home
No telemetry and no account with anyone. It only reaches out for prices, for push messages and for a hosted model, and only if you turn them on.

Who it is not for

Bussola is not for everyone, and it is better to know now.

  • One bank account and no cash? Your bank's app already shows you everything. You do not need this.
  • You want transactions to arrive by themselves from the bank. Bussola has no open banking sync, on purpose: it never holds a bank login. It reads your bank's notifications and its CSV files instead, and you confirm each movement.
  • You budget with envelopes or zero-based. There are no budgets, rules or recurring transactions. Bussola shows what you actually spent, category by category and month by month, and the planned expenses pillar keeps the money for known costs apart.
  • You trade actively. A holding has one purchase price, a return and an annualised return; there is no trade-by-trade history or dividend tracking. Ghostfolio does that well.
  • You run a business. No invoicing, no double-entry accounting, no tax reports.
  • You cannot or will not run Docker. There is no hosted version: Bussola runs on a machine of yours, or nowhere.

Run it yourself

Three containers and one port, on a Raspberry Pi, a NAS or any machine with Docker. About five minutes.

mkdir bussola && cd bussola
curl -LO https://github.com/marc0todar0/bussola/releases/latest/download/docker-compose.yml
curl -L -o .env https://github.com/marc0todar0/bussola/releases/latest/download/example.env
# set your username and passwords in .env
docker compose up -d

Then open http://localhost:3000. The README links the guides to HTTPS, passkeys, price updates, bank notifications and upgrades.
